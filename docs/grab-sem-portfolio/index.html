How I work

Five areas I focus on as an engineering manager, with examples from my time leading maps engineering at Grab.

01
Technical Strategy

Turning long technical bets into shipped work

I own the platform roadmap for consumer maps: large migrations such as Kotlin 2.1 and Android 16 (API 36), a navigation re-architecture on MapLibre, and cleanup of experimentation infrastructure. My job is to break multi-quarter technical work into pieces that ship without stalling product work.

The surfaces under my teams include the home-screen map, trip planning, and place search and discovery in one of Southeast Asia's largest consumer apps.

Platform roadmap owner Map, trip planning, search & discovery
02
Growing Leaders

Building a team that can run without me

I lead a team of 12 engineers across Singapore, Beijing, and Jakarta, and have grown it through hiring. I've coached four engineers into leads who fully own their product areas.

I set up a navigation team in Jakarta from scratch and wrote the onboarding guides that get new hires shipping production code in their first week or two.

12 engineers · 4 leads grown New team stood up in Jakarta
03
Engineering Excellence

Taking on the hard migrations myself

As a first-time manager in late 2022, I took over a crowdsourced map-contribution platform that was struggling, and later helped build Grab's in-house navigation stack, which now runs in the driver app. I personally shipped the Kotlin 2 (K2 compiler) and Android 16 migration on a core map SDK: KAPT to KSP, edge-to-edge UI, and several dependency upgrades with the full test suite passing.

I designed the unified feature-flag approach for consumer maps and led a multi-year, cross-platform build of a map-contribution SDK for Android and iOS. I review for release safety on both the passenger and driver release trains, and I dig into the hardest native crashes, including memory-corruption bugs in the C++ map renderer.

Kotlin 2 · KSP · edge-to-edge Android + iOS SDKs Native C++ crash debugging
04
Cross-Team Partnership

A bridge between maps and the rest of the app

Maps sit underneath almost every product in a super app, so a lot of my work is coordination. I work with transport, food, payments, driver, and customer-support teams to get map features into their flows.

One example: a collaboration that surfaces restaurant deals on the map during a ride, built with the food and transport teams, which we then wrote up as a reusable model for future cross-team projects.

Transport · Food · Payments · Driver Reusable collaboration blueprint
05
Operational Rigor

Calm incident response and honest retros

When production issues hit navigation or the driver app, I coordinate the response: reproduction with QA, feature-flag rollback, recovery, and clear communication to leadership.

Afterwards I run blameless retros and turn them into process fixes, such as better severity classification and faster on-call escalation.

Incident command Blameless retros
AI-native engineering

Agentic tooling as a team habit

I treat AI coding agents as part of the team's toolkit and build the tooling that makes them useful on a large mobile codebase.

Tools I've built

  • An AI coding orchestrator for Android, iOS, and backend work, now used by the whole team.

  • An MCP server for generating QR codes and deep links, so operations teams can create campaign links without waiting on engineers.
  • A team Gen AI workshop guide for setting up Claude Code, Cursor, and MCP.
